# Kerno NIS2 Operating Cycle

Sales-deck outline for a founder-delivered NIS2 file: one critical service,
10–20 priority controls, live in Kerno in 30 days. A paid diagnostic, then a
six-month monthly cycle. An alternative to hiring a dedicated resource solely
to operate this bounded evidence and oversight cycle — not a CISO office.

**Audience:** CEO, managing director, board member, general counsel, or CFO of a
German organisation that is, or may be, an important or particularly important
entity under NIS2 and the BSI Act.

**Not for the customer:** the internal commercial note at the end. Everything
above that line is speakable.

---

## Purpose and legal framing

The core proposition:

> A consultant can help management prepare for an audit. Kerno’s operating
> cycle helps management make the next required decision on one critical
> service, record it while it is happening, and show the evidence it rested
> on — without hiring someone solely to keep that bounded file alive.

Article 20 of the NIS2 Directive requires management bodies to approve
cybersecurity risk-management measures, oversee implementation, and undergo
training; it also requires Member States to provide for management
accountability
([EUR-Lex, NIS2 Article 20](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32022L2555)).
Under the current German framework, §38 BSIG places responsibility on
management for implementing and monitoring §30 risk-management measures and
requires regular management training. The BSI explains that operational
execution and monitoring may be delegated, but overall responsibility and
appropriate control and supervisory duties remain with management
([BSI management briefing](https://www.bsi.bund.de/SharedDocs/Downloads/DE/BSI/Publikationen/Broschueren/Management_Blitzlicht/Management_Blitzlicht_NIS-2.pdf?__blob=publicationFile&v=6)).

**Statutory non-sufficiency (say this before any other promise):**

> This service does not determine NIS2 applicability or establish compliance
> with §30 or §38 BSIG. It maintains a traceable operating record for one
> defined service and control subset within the customer’s wider entity-level
> obligations.

The BSI describes §30 risk management as covering the systems, components,
and processes used to provide the organisation’s services. One selected
service cannot be presented as the complete statutory programme.

“Non-delegable duties” in this deck means that management may delegate
execution but cannot outsource its ultimate accountability. This service does
not replace management, a control owner, or a full-time CISO. It does not
discharge the training duty. It does not handle incidents, legal assessment,
or statutory notification. Final customer-facing legal claims and contractual
language should be reviewed by German counsel for the prospect’s legal form,
sector, and facts.

## Core positioning

**Category:** Founder-delivered NIS2 operating cycle: named operator plus the
Kerno copilot, scoped to one entity, one critical service, and 10–20
priority controls.

**Not:** A CISO-office retainer, a multi-entity GRC platform, a training
programme, a statutory-compliance determination, a one-time PDF assessment,
or a document repository.

**This offer actually delivers:** evidence collection and mapping, a gap and
exception register, human-reviewed recommendations, a monthly management
review, one recorded management decision, and an exportable evidence pack.
Go-live means the tenant is live, evidence is linked, the register is
visible, and the first monthly review has been held — not that the
organisation meets §30 or §38.

**Promise:** For the in-scope service and control subset, Kerno maps
controls, links named evidence, scores what is present, and retains human
review and named decisions with timestamps. The operator keeps the gap
register current and runs the monthly review from that live record.
Management still decides. The customer’s wider entity-level obligations
remain the customer’s.

**What may be said in the room, until the claim tests in the internal note
are complete:**

> For in-scope records created and retained in Kerno, the platform maintains
> an audit trail designed to preserve evidence links, scores, human review,
> named decisions, and timestamps.

**What must not be said:** that this cycle establishes NIS2 or BSIG
compliance; that Kerno proves exactly how every compliance decision was
made; that every green status is independently tested; that original signed
files are retained; that expired risk acceptances reopen themselves; that
10–20 controls are the NIS2 programme; that management training is part of
this fee; that Kerno detects, responds to, or notifies incidents; or that
management may waive a mandatory legal requirement by “accepting a gap.”

**Executive value proposition:**

> Kerno does not remove management liability and does not determine
> statutory sufficiency. For one critical service, it gives management a
> monthly exception cadence and a contemporaneous operating record so the
> next in-scope decision is not reconstructed after the fact.

### Slide 6: Monthly oversight, not an annual green slide

**Headline:** The meeting is exception-led. Closures need named evidence.

**Operating cycle:**

1. **Collect:** Control owners submit evidence through Kerno (upload) or a
   pre-agreed inbound connector. Originals remain with the customer.
2. **Link, score, and provenance:** Each record is linked to named controls
   with the provenance packet. Coverage is scored from those links.
3. **Register:** Gaps and exceptions have an owner, an age, and either
   linked evidence or an explicit gap. This register is the agenda.
4. **Review:** Monthly 90-minute session with management or the delegated
   sponsor. Only items that need a decision or a push. Recommendations on
   the table have already been human-reviewed.
5. **Preserve:** Decisions in that meeting are recorded in Kerno.
   Remediation claims are not closed on a verbal update; they need linked
   evidence.

**Evidence produced:**

- Source-linked evidence, provenance packet, and the time text was
  extracted
- Coverage status and confidence per in-scope control
- Exception owner, age, and whether closure has evidence
- Monthly review note and named decisions
- Exportable evidence pack for the in-scope family

“Traditional oversight relies on self-reporting and slides built the week

### Slide 8: Provenance, the chain, and the claim we will stand behind

**Headline:** Every in-scope status should resolve to a named record. Original
files stay with you. Extracted text is what is scored.

**On-slide flow:**

**Critical service → 10–20 priority controls → linked evidence (text +
provenance packet) → score → human-reviewed recommendation → exception
register → named human decision → retained record → evidence pack**

**Design principle:** No status in the monthly pack without a control, a
score, and either linked evidence or an explicit gap.

**Original files are not stored in Kerno.** Extracted text does not preserve
signatures, all metadata, or full authenticity of the source document. The
customer retains the original. For every in-scope evidence item, Kerno and
the operator together retain enough provenance to show which original
produced the scored text:

| Provenance field | Where it lives |
| --- | --- |
| Customer-controlled source reference | Captured by the operator (canonical location: SharePoint, DMS, ticket, or owner path) |
| Original filename | `external_id` / title, truncated to the column limit |
| Document version | Captured by the operator on the evidence item |
| Extraction timestamp | `created_at` / `fetched_at` |
| Content hash of the extracted text | SHA-256 `content_hash` |
| Evidence owner | Captured by the operator on the item or register |
| Canonical storage location | Captured by the operator; the original stays there |
| Extracted text used for scoring | `body` |

Without that packet, the item is not in-scope evidence. It is an upload
waiting to be completed.

**What we can show today, for in-scope records created and retained in
Kerno:**

- A link from an in-scope control to the evidence used to score it
- A documented score and confidence level
- Human review of the recommendation before it is treated as a decision
- A named person and timestamp on approve / edit / reject
- An audit trail designed to preserve those facts
- An exportable evidence pack for the in-scope family

**What we will not claim until the tests in the internal note pass, and
will not claim even then outside in-scope records:**

- That “every recommendation” and “every human decision” in the abstract
  are traceable
- That scores are reproducible in all conditions
- That the ledger is tamper-evident against every administrative path
- That every status in the estate resolves to evidence
- That history cannot be silently altered
- That exports reproduce every underlying relationship after termination
  and restore
- That original PDFs are on file
- That 10–20 controls prove NIS2 conformity

**Speaker script:**

“A dashboard without provenance is another assertion. Inside this cycle,
green, amber, or red is a scored control with named evidence or a named gap.
The text we scored is hashed. The original stays in your system, with a
pointer we keep. A recommendation has been reviewed by a human. A decision
has a person and a timestamp. That is the operating record. It is not a
substitute for the signed original, and it is not a substitute for your
statutory programme. We will not tell your counsel a stronger story than
that.”

## Objection handling

### “We already have an ISO 27001 certification.”

**Response:** “ISO 27001 may be a useful management-system foundation, but
the BSI states that certification does not by itself prove NIS2 or BSIG
conformity and may cover the statutory requirements only incompletely
([BSI on ISO 27001 and NIS2](https://www.bsi.bund.de/DE/Themen/Regulierte-Wirtschaft/NIS-2-regulierte-Unternehmen/NIS-2-Infopakete/NIS-2-ISO27001/NIS-2-ISO-27001.html)).
We will use existing ISO evidence rather than duplicate it, then run the
in-scope controls against linked evidence. Certification is not this cycle,
and this cycle is not a NIS2 determination.”

### “Our CISO already handles this.”

**Response:** “Then this offer is the wrong purchase. The CISO can operate
the programme; management still holds §38. If the CISO’s problem is the
file — evidence, scores, named decisions — that is a later product
conversation. It is not a second operator at €5,500. We will not sell you
a headcount you do not need.”

### “Our auditor checks this annually.”

**Response:** “An annual audit samples a point in time. This cycle makes
the in-scope file easier to show between audits. It does not replace the
audit, and it does not replace entity-level oversight under §38.”

### “We can store this in SharePoint and Jira.”

**Response:** “Those tools should hold your originals — we are not a
document repository. They do not, by themselves, score coverage from linked
evidence, keep a named human decision on the control, or export the pack

attributed, you do not need us.”

### “Software cannot protect directors from liability.”

**Response:** “Correct. Neither can an operator. We help management perform
the next in-scope decision systematically and keep a contemporaneous
operating record. That is the whole claim. We do not establish compliance
and we do not remove liability.”

### “Why not buy the platform without the operator?”

**Response:** “Because this offer exists for organisations that were not
