# Kerno NIS2 Operating Cycle

Sales-deck outline for a founder-delivered management cadence. One regulated
entity. One critical service. Priced under a CISO hire, not as a CISO office.

**Audience:** CEO, managing director, board member, general counsel, or CFO of a
German organisation that is, or may be, an important or particularly important
entity under NIS2 and the BSI Act.

**Not for the customer:** the internal commercial note at the end. Everything
above that line is speakable.

---

## Purpose and legal framing

The core proposition:

> A consultant can help management prepare for an audit. Kerno’s operating
> cycle helps management make the next required decision, record it while it
> is happening, and show the evidence it rested on — for one service, every
> month, without hiring a CISO to run the paperwork.

Article 20 of the NIS2 Directive requires management bodies to approve
cybersecurity risk-management measures, oversee implementation, and undergo
training; it also requires Member States to provide for management
accountability
([EUR-Lex, NIS2 Article 20](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32022L2555)).
Under the current German framework, §38 BSIG places responsibility on
management for implementing and monitoring §30 risk-management measures and
requires regular management training. The BSI explains that operational
execution and monitoring may be delegated, but overall responsibility and
appropriate control and supervisory duties remain with management
([BSI management briefing](https://www.bsi.bund.de/SharedDocs/Downloads/DE/BSI/Publikationen/Broschueren/Management_Blitzlicht/Management_Blitzlicht_NIS-2.pdf?__blob=publicationFile&v=6)).

“Non-delegable duties” in this deck means that management may delegate
execution but cannot outsource its ultimate accountability. This service does
not replace management, a control owner, or a full-time CISO. It runs the
recurring cycle those people otherwise fail to keep. Final customer-facing
legal claims and contractual language should be reviewed by German counsel
for the prospect’s legal form, sector, and facts.

## Core positioning

**Category:** Founder-delivered NIS2 operating cycle: named operator plus the
Kerno copilot, scoped to one entity and one critical service.

**Not:** A CISO-office retainer, a multi-entity GRC platform, a training LMS,
a one-time readiness assessment, or a document repository.

**Promise:** Kerno maps the service’s controls to NIS2, links named evidence,
scores what is present, and retains every human decision with a name and a
timestamp. The operator prepares the monthly exception review and the
quarterly approval packet from that live record. Management still decides.

**What is true of the product today, and may be said in the room:**

> Every recommendation and every human decision made in Kerno is traceable to
> named evidence, a reproducible score, a named human, and a timestamp —
> with tamper-evident, database-enforced logging of every human decision.

**What is not yet true, and must not be said:** that Kerno proves exactly how
every compliance decision was made; that every green status is independently
tested; that original signed files are retained; that expired risk
acceptances reopen themselves; or that management training is a product.

**Executive value proposition:**

> Kerno does not remove management liability. For one critical service, it
> gives management a monthly cadence and a contemporaneous record so
> approval, oversight, and the next decision are not reconstructed after the
> fact.

### Slide 5: Duty one — informed approval

**Headline:** Replace ceremonial sign-off with one decision-grade packet per
quarter.

**Operating cycle (one critical service):**

1. **Trigger:** The quarterly forum, or a material change the operator
   brings forward (new supplier, incident, failed control, expired
   acceptance).
2. **Assemble:** The operator builds an approval packet from Kerno’s live
   control map, linked evidence, scores, open exceptions, and prior
   decisions. The packet names the service, the NIS2 measures in scope, what
   is evidenced, what is not, and the residual risk being put to management.
3. **Challenge:** The operator records questions, dissent, and required
   revisions in the packet and in Kerno as a named human decision.
4. **Decide:** Authorised management approves, rejects, conditionally
   approves, or accepts residual risk with an expiry date.
5. **Bind:** Kerno retains who decided, when, on which recommendation and
   evidence set. The operator files the packet and the expiry date against
   the next forum.
6. **Reopen:** The operator brings expired or invalidated decisions back.
   Automatic reopen is not a product feature in this offer.

**Evidence produced:**

- Approval packet (operator-authored, stored against the service)
- Named approver, date, and Kerno decision record
- Linked evidence and the score at the time of the review
- Conditions, follow-up owners, and residual-risk expiry on the operator
  register

**Speaker script:**

“A signature on a policy proves that someone clicked approve. It does not
prove the decision was informed. Each quarter we put one packet in front of
you: the service, the measures, the live evidence, the gaps, and the residual
risk. You decide. Kerno keeps who decided and on what. We do not pretend the
platform wrote the packet. The operator did, from a record that is already
there.”

### Slide 6: Duty two — active oversight

**Headline:** Oversight is a monthly exception meeting, not an annual green
slide.

**Operating cycle:**

1. **Collect:** Control owners submit evidence through Kerno (upload) or
   existing inbound connectors. Original files are not retained; extracted
   text is. Say that once, clearly.
2. **Link and score:** Each record is linked to named controls. Coverage is
   scored from those links — not from a separate unaudited dashboard.
3. **Surface:** The operator reviews met / partial / gap, unlinked evidence,
   and open remediations before the monthly meeting.
4. **Escalate:** Material gaps and overdue closures go to the named owner,
   then into the monthly review. Jira can carry the task where the client
   already works that way.
5. **Review:** Monthly 90-minute exception review with management or the
   delegated sponsor. Only items that need a decision or a push.
6. **Preserve:** Human decisions in that meeting are recorded in Kerno.
   Remediation claims are not closed on a verbal update; they need linked
   evidence.

**Evidence produced:**

- Source-linked evidence and the time it was recorded
- Coverage status and confidence per in-scope control
- Exception owner, age, and whether closure has evidence
- Monthly review note and named decisions

**Speaker script:**

“Traditional oversight relies on self-reporting and a meeting whose slides
were built the week before. We change the cadence: Kerno already knows which
controls have evidence and which do not. The monthly review is exception-led.
If a closure cannot be pointed at a named record, it is not closed. That is
narrower than a full monitoring platform, and it is already enough to stop
the usual drift between audits.”

## Objection handling

### “We already have an ISO 27001 certification.”

**Response:** “ISO 27001 may be a useful management-system foundation, but
the BSI states that certification does not by itself prove NIS2 or BSIG
conformity and may cover the statutory requirements only incompletely
([BSI on ISO 27001 and NIS2](https://www.bsi.bund.de/DE/Themen/Regulierte-Wirtschaft/NIS-2-regulierte-Unternehmen/NIS-2-Infopakete/NIS-2-ISO27001/NIS-2-ISO-27001.html)).
We will use existing ISO evidence rather than duplicate it, then run
approval and oversight against the in-scope NIS2 measures. Certification is
not the cycle.”

### “Our CISO already handles this.”

**Response:** “Then this offer is probably the wrong purchase. The CISO can
operate the programme; management still holds §38. If the CISO’s problem is
the file — evidence, scores, named decisions — that is a later product
conversation. It is not a second operator at €6,500. We will not sell you a
headcount you do not need.”

### “Our auditor checks this annually.”

**Response:** “An annual audit samples a point in time. Oversight under §38
has to remain informed as controls change. This cycle makes the audit
easier. Its job is the eleven months in between.”

### “We can store this in SharePoint and Jira.”

**Response:** “Those tools hold files and tasks. They do not score coverage
from linked evidence, keep a named human decision on the control, or export
the pack from the same record. If your file in SharePoint is already current
and attributed, you do not need us. If it is not, adding another folder will
not create the cadence.”

### “Software cannot protect directors from liability.”

**Response:** “Correct. Neither can an operator. We help management perform
the next decision systematically and keep contemporaneous proof. That is the
whole claim.”

### “Why not buy the platform without the operator?”

**Response:** “Because this offer exists for organisations that were not
going to operate it. The copilot without a cadence becomes another dashboard.
When we have a self-serve product we are willing to sell on its own, we will
price that separately. We will not unbundle this cycle into a cheap licence
that leaves you running the meetings.”

### “€6,500 is a lot for a tool.”

**Response:** “It is the wrong comparison. The tool without the operator is
not this offer. The right comparisons are a loaded CISO hire, a repeating
consulting project, and the internal time currently spent rebuilding the

## Recommended recurring cadence

| Cadence | Operator | Kerno | Management |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Between meetings | Chase missing evidence; prepare exceptions | Coverage, links, scores, inbound evidence | Nothing, unless a threshold exception is raised |
| Monthly | 90-minute exception review | Live coverage and open decisions | Challenge owners, assign closures |
| Quarterly | Approval packet and residual-risk register | Named decision record and evidence pack | Approve, reject, condition, or time-limit |
| Event | Bring a material incident or supplier failure to the next review | Preserve the then-current evidence and decisions | Direct the response; do not wait for the quarter if the operator flags it |
| Pilot end | Effectiveness readout against the metrics below | Export of the in-scope record | Continue at the same rate, or stop |

## Suggested outcome metrics

Use these to prove the cycle is working. Do not reduce compliance to a
single score. Do not report metrics the copilot cannot support.

- Percentage of in-scope controls with current linked evidence
- Count of unlinked evidence records (orphans made visible)
- Median age of open high exceptions
- Percentage of remediation closures that have linked evidence
- Days from packet to recorded management decision
- Number of in-scope controls still scored gap
- Management briefing attendance for the covered directors
- One evidence-pack export successfully produced

Do not promise: independent verification rates, automatically reopened
acceptances, or “unsupported green statuses eliminated across the estate.”
Those belong to a later product.

## Internal commercial note — not for the customer deck

### Can we actually command €6,500 per month?

**Yes, as list, for a narrow buyer. It is not a gimme and it is not a
software price.**

€6,500/month is €39,000 for the six-month pilot, €47,000 with activation, or
€78,000 if they convert to a year. That is:

| Comparison | Typical DACH range | What €6,500 looks like |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Loaded mid-market CISO | €140–220k, plus 4–9 months to hire | Clearly cheaper; this is the intended frame |
| GRC / ISMS manager FTE | €90–130k loaded | Not cheaper. Do not sell against this hire |
| Fractional vCISO, 2 days/month | €5–10k/month, often with no system of record | Overlap. We win only if Kerno is visible in every meeting |
| NIS2 project (boutique) | €30–80k, then nothing | We are more expensive in year one, cheaper if they would have bought a second project |
| Mid-market GRC SaaS licence | ~€12–24k **per year** | We lose this comparison. Never use it in the room |

So €6,500 is commandable when the alternative in the buyer’s head is “we
need someone to run NIS2” — a Geschäftsführer at a 50–400 person German
entity, no CISO, already in the BSI window, tired of a binder. It is not
commandable when the alternative is “we need a tool” or “we already have a
CISO.”

**Hold €6,500 if all of the following are true:**

1. The buyer was heading toward a CISO or retained vCISO, not toward a
   software RFP.
2. Every monthly review shows Kerno artifacts (coverage, linked evidence,
   named decisions, export) — otherwise they will renegotiate toward a
   €4–5k fractional.
3. The contract is a six-month pilot, not a twelve-month leap of faith on a
   pre-PMF product.
4. We cap delivery at two, at most three, concurrent cycles. This offer
   exists to replace founder salary while the GRC product is built. A fourth
   client turns us into a consultancy and stops the product.

**If they push on price:** cut controls or drop one of the two quarterly
forums in the pilot. Floor before we should walk: **€4,500/month** for a
clearly reduced scope (one service, ≤15 controls, monthly review kept).
Below that we are a day-rate consultant with a logo, and the copilot is a
prop.

**If they want annual prepay:** 5% on six months is enough; do not trade
rate for cash.

### Why this is the offer

The previous three-tier “Executive Defense” retainer at €14,500/month
described a GRC operating system and a vCISO bench we do not have. Gross
margin on that book would be consulting, not software, and the founder would
be the bench. This cycle is intentionally smaller: enough cash to fund the
build, honest about what Kerno does today, and capped so the product still
ships.

Expansion (more services, training product, automated reopen, self-serve
licence) is earned after two pilots that complete, not sold from this deck.
