# Kerno NIS2 Operating Cycle

Sales-deck outline for a founder-delivered NIS2 file: one critical service,
10–20 priority controls, live in Kerno in 30 days. A paid diagnostic, then a
six-month monthly cycle. An alternative to hiring a dedicated resource
solely to operate this bounded cycle — not a CISO office.

**Audience:** CEO, managing director, board member, general counsel, or CFO of a
German organisation that is, or may be, an important or particularly important
entity under NIS2 and the BSI Act.

**Not for the customer:** the internal commercial note at the end. Everything
above that line is speakable.

---

## Purpose and legal framing

The core proposition:

> A consultant can help management prepare for an audit. Kerno’s operating
> cycle helps management make the next required decision on one critical
> service, record it while it is happening, and show the evidence it rested
> on — without hiring someone solely to keep that file alive.

Article 20 of the NIS2 Directive requires management bodies to approve
cybersecurity risk-management measures, oversee implementation, and undergo
training; it also requires Member States to provide for management
accountability
([EUR-Lex, NIS2 Article 20](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32022L2555)).
Under the current German framework, §38 BSIG places responsibility on
management for implementing and monitoring §30 risk-management measures and
requires regular management training. The BSI explains that operational
execution and monitoring may be delegated, but overall responsibility and
appropriate control and supervisory duties remain with management
([BSI management briefing](https://www.bsi.bund.de/SharedDocs/Downloads/DE/BSI/Publikationen/Broschueren/Management_Blitzlicht/Management_Blitzlicht_NIS-2.pdf?__blob=publicationFile&v=6)).

“Non-delegable duties” in this deck means that management may delegate
execution but cannot outsource its ultimate accountability. This service does
not replace management, a control owner, or a full-time CISO. It does not
discharge the training duty. It runs oversight operations and records named
decisions for a tightly bounded control set.

Say this once, on the limits slide and in the contract — not as the opening
of the pitch:

> This service does not determine NIS2 applicability or establish compliance
> with §30 or §38 BSIG. It maintains a traceable operating record for one
> defined service and control subset within the customer’s wider entity-level
> obligations.

One selected service is not the complete statutory programme. Final
customer-facing legal claims and contractual language should be reviewed by
German counsel for the prospect’s legal form, sector, and facts.

## Core positioning

**Category:** Founder-delivered NIS2 operating cycle: named operator plus the
Kerno copilot, scoped to one entity, one critical service, and 10–20
priority controls.

**Not:** A CISO-office retainer, a multi-entity GRC platform, a training
programme, a one-time PDF assessment, or a document repository.

**This offer actually delivers:** evidence collection and mapping, a gap and
exception register, human-reviewed recommendations, a monthly management
review, one recorded management decision, and an exportable evidence pack.
Go-live means the tenant is live, evidence is linked, the register is
visible, and the first monthly review has been held — not that the
organisation meets §30 or §38.

**Promise:** Kerno maps the service’s priority controls to NIS2, links named
evidence, scores what is present, and retains every human decision with a
name and a timestamp. The operator keeps the gap register current and runs
the monthly review from that live record. Management still decides.

**What is true of the product today, and may be said in the room:**

> Every recommendation and every human decision made in Kerno is traceable to
> named evidence, a reproducible score, a named human, and a timestamp —
> with tamper-evident, database-enforced logging of every human decision.

**What is not yet true, and must not be said:** that this cycle establishes
NIS2 or BSIG compliance; that Kerno proves exactly how every compliance
decision was made; that every green status is independently tested; that
original signed files are retained; that expired risk acceptances reopen
themselves; that 10–20 controls are the NIS2 programme; that management
training is part of this fee; that Kerno detects, responds to, or notifies
incidents; or that recording a residual-risk treatment waives a mandatory
legal requirement.

The approved in-scope claim above is the product demo sentence, scoped to
records created in Kerno. It is not a statutory-sufficiency claim. Do not
replace it with weaker “designed to preserve” hedging unless counsel asks.

**Executive value proposition:**

> Kerno does not remove management liability. For one critical service, it
> gives management a monthly exception cadence and a contemporaneous record
> so the next decision is not reconstructed after the fact.

### Slide 6: Monthly oversight, not an annual green slide

**Headline:** The meeting is exception-led. Closures need named evidence.

**Operating cycle:**

1. **Collect:** Control owners submit evidence through Kerno (upload) or
   existing inbound connectors.
2. **Link and score:** Each record is linked to named controls. Coverage is
   scored from those links.
3. **Register:** Gaps and exceptions have an owner, an age, and either
   linked evidence or an explicit gap. This register is the agenda.
4. **Review:** Monthly 90-minute session with management or the delegated
   sponsor. Only items that need a decision or a push. Recommendations on
   the table have already been human-reviewed.
5. **Preserve:** Decisions in that meeting are recorded in Kerno.
   Remediation claims are not closed on a verbal update; they need linked
   evidence.

**Evidence produced:**

- Source-linked evidence and the time it was recorded
- Coverage status and confidence per in-scope control
- Exception owner, age, and whether closure has evidence
- Monthly review note and named decisions
- Exportable evidence pack for the in-scope family

**Speaker script:**

“Traditional oversight relies on self-reporting and slides built the week
before. We change the cadence: Kerno already knows which of the in-scope
controls have evidence and which do not. The monthly review is the register.
If a closure cannot be pointed at a named record, it is not closed. That is
narrower than a monitoring platform, and it is already enough to stop the
usual drift between audits — on this service, for these controls.”

for implementing and monitoring the required measures. The BSI is explicit
that operational execution may be delegated, but overall responsibility
remains with management.

We are not selling you a CISO, and we are not selling you a training
programme or an incident function. We run oversight operations for one
critical service: evidence, gaps, human-reviewed recommendations, a monthly
review, and a named record of the in-scope decisions you make. That is not
a §30 or §38 determination.”

### Problem

“Traditional GRC consulting is a project. The consultant assesses, writes,
and leaves. Drift starts the following week. Evidence goes stale. Exceptions
stay open. The questions that matter after an incident — what did management
know, on what evidence, who accepted what until when — were never written
down.

A hire can close that gap, at salary cost, after a search. A tool can store
files, if someone internally operates it. Most organisations in this room
have neither. They have last year’s report.”

### Solution

“Kerno is the copilot: NIS2 control map, linked evidence, a reproducible
score, human-reviewed recommendations, named decisions, a tamper-evident
ledger, an exportable pack. A named operator — founder-delivered — stands
that file up in two weeks and runs the monthly exception review from it.
Management decides. We do not take accountability. We stop the file from
being reconstructed.”

### Commercial justification

“€9,000 is a two-week diagnostic that already lives in Kerno. You keep your
originals whether or not you continue, and you receive an export if you
stop. €5,500 a month is a six-month operating cost to keep the meeting and
the register honest. It is an alternative to hiring a dedicated resource
solely to operate this bounded cycle. Optional connectors are priced before
we start, or they wait. If the file is not real at day 30, you still have a
recorded decision and an export; you do not have a three-year platform
contract, and you do not have a compliance certificate.”

### Close

“Pick the service and the decision. We will not start with a generic demo.
If the pack and the record are not better than what you have, do not buy
the rest.”

## Objection handling

### “We already have an ISO 27001 certification.”

**Response:** “ISO 27001 may be a useful management-system foundation, but
the BSI states that certification does not by itself prove NIS2 or BSIG
conformity and may cover the statutory requirements only incompletely
([BSI on ISO 27001 and NIS2](https://www.bsi.bund.de/DE/Themen/Regulierte-Wirtschaft/NIS-2-regulierte-Unternehmen/NIS-2-Infopakete/NIS-2-ISO27001/NIS-2-ISO-27001.html)).
We will use existing ISO evidence rather than duplicate it, then run the
in-scope controls against linked evidence. Certification is not the cycle.”

### “Our CISO already handles this.”

**Response:** “Then this offer is the wrong purchase. The CISO can operate
the programme; management still holds §38. If the CISO’s problem is the
file — evidence, scores, named decisions — that is a later product
conversation. It is not a second operator at €5,500. We will not sell you
a headcount you do not need.”

### “Our auditor checks this annually.”

**Response:** “An annual audit samples a point in time. Oversight under §38
has to remain informed as controls change. This cycle makes the audit
easier. Its job is the eleven months in between, on the in-scope service.”

### “We can store this in SharePoint and Jira.”

**Response:** “Those tools hold files and tasks. They do not score coverage
from linked evidence, keep a named human decision on the control, or export
the pack from the same record. If your file in SharePoint is already current
and attributed, you do not need us. If it is not, adding another folder will
not create the cadence.”

### “Software cannot protect directors from liability.”

**Response:** “Correct. Neither can an operator. We help management perform
the next in-scope decision systematically and keep contemporaneous proof.
That is the whole claim.”

### “Why not buy the platform without the operator?”

**Response:** “Because this offer exists for organisations that were not
going to operate it. The copilot without a cadence becomes another dashboard.
When we have a self-serve product we are willing to sell on its own, we will
price that separately. We will not unbundle this cycle into a cheap licence
that leaves you running the meetings.”

### “€5,500 is a lot for a tool.” / “€9,000 is a lot for a workshop.”

**Response:** “Wrong comparison. €9,000 is a live file, not a workshop.
€5,500 is the meeting and the register, not a licence. The right comparisons
are hiring someone to run this file, repeating a consulting project, and the
internal time currently spent rebuilding the binder. If those are not the
alternative you were considering, we should stop.”

### “Can you do this for the whole group?”

**Response:** “Not as this offer. One entity, one service, 10–20 priority
controls. If the six months work, we can talk about a second service. We
will not sign a multi-entity programme we cannot deliver.”

### “Does this include management training?”

**Response:** “No. Training remains your duty under Article 20 and §38. We
will not pretend a briefing inside this fee discharges it. If you want that
scoped separately after the diagnostic, we can talk. It is not in the
€5,500.”

### “Why would we pay to start and then pay again to onboard?”

**Response:** “You would not. The diagnostic includes owners and the
calendar. The only extra is optional integration enablement, and only if we
agreed it in writing before the diagnostic was signed.”

### “What happens if we have an incident?”

**Response:** “You activate your own incident-response, legal, and reporting
procedures immediately. Kerno is not that service. We do not wait for the
next monthly meeting, because we are not in the detection or notification
path. Where relevant, a later review can record the governance decisions
and control changes that followed.”

### “Can we just accept the gaps?”

**Response:** “No. Management sets treatment, owner, target date, and
interim safeguards. Recording that in Kerno does not waive a mandatory
legal requirement.”

### “What do we have if we stop?”

**Response:** “Your originals, and an export of the in-scope file. Not a
self-serve login. Retention and deletion are in the DPA.”

| --- | --- | --- | --- |

## Suggested outcome metrics

Use these to prove the cycle is working. Do not reduce compliance to a
single score. Do not report metrics the copilot cannot support.

- Percentage of in-scope controls with current linked evidence
- Count of unlinked evidence records (orphans made visible)
- Number of open exceptions with a named owner
- Median age of open high exceptions
- Percentage of remediation closures that have linked evidence
- Percentage of open recommendations that have been human-reviewed
- Days from pack to recorded management decision
- Number of in-scope controls still scored gap
- One evidence-pack export successfully produced

Do not promise: independent verification rates, automatically reopened
acceptances, training completion, or “unsupported green statuses eliminated
across the estate.” Those belong to a later product.
