# Kerno NIS2 Operating Cycle

Sales-deck outline for a founder-delivered NIS2 file: one critical service,
10–20 priority controls, live in Kerno in 30 days. A paid diagnostic, then a
six-month monthly cycle. Priced under a file-running hire, not as a CISO
office.

**Audience:** CEO, managing director, board member, general counsel, or CFO of a
German organisation that is, or may be, an important or particularly important
entity under NIS2 and the BSI Act.

**Not for the customer:** the internal commercial note at the end. Everything
above that line is speakable.

---

## Purpose and legal framing

The core proposition:

> A consultant can help management prepare for an audit. Kerno’s operating
> cycle helps management make the next required decision on one critical
> service, record it while it is happening, and show the evidence it rested
> on — without hiring someone solely to keep that file alive.

Article 20 of the NIS2 Directive requires management bodies to approve
cybersecurity risk-management measures, oversee implementation, and undergo
training; it also requires Member States to provide for management
accountability
([EUR-Lex, NIS2 Article 20](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32022L2555)).
Under the current German framework, §38 BSIG places responsibility on
management for implementing and monitoring §30 risk-management measures and
requires regular management training. The BSI explains that operational
execution and monitoring may be delegated, but overall responsibility and
appropriate control and supervisory duties remain with management
([BSI management briefing](https://www.bsi.bund.de/SharedDocs/Downloads/DE/BSI/Publikationen/Broschueren/Management_Blitzlicht/Management_Blitzlicht_NIS-2.pdf?__blob=publicationFile&v=6)).

“Non-delegable duties” in this deck means that management may delegate
execution but cannot outsource its ultimate accountability. This service does
not replace management, a control owner, or a full-time CISO. It does not
discharge the training duty. It runs oversight operations and records named
decisions for a tightly bounded control set. Final customer-facing legal
claims and contractual language should be reviewed by German counsel for the
prospect’s legal form, sector, and facts.

## Core positioning

**Category:** Founder-delivered NIS2 operating cycle: named operator plus the
Kerno copilot, scoped to one entity, one critical service, and 10–20
priority controls.

**Not:** A CISO-office retainer, a multi-entity GRC platform, a training
programme, a one-time PDF assessment, or a document repository.

**This offer actually delivers:** evidence collection and mapping, a gap and
exception register, human-reviewed recommendations, a monthly management
review, one recorded management decision, and an exportable evidence pack.
Go-live means the tenant is live, evidence is linked, the register is
visible, and the first monthly review has been held — not that the
organisation is defensible.

**Promise:** Kerno maps the service’s priority controls to NIS2, links named
evidence, scores what is present, and retains every human decision with a
name and a timestamp. The operator keeps the gap register current and runs
the monthly review from that live record. Management still decides.

**What is true of the product today, and may be said in the room:**

> Every recommendation and every human decision made in Kerno is traceable to
> named evidence, a reproducible score, a named human, and a timestamp —
> with tamper-evident, database-enforced logging of every human decision.

**What is not yet true, and must not be said:** that Kerno proves exactly how
every compliance decision was made; that every green status is independently
tested; that original signed files are retained; that expired risk
acceptances reopen themselves; that 10–20 controls are the NIS2 programme;
or that management training is part of this fee.

**Executive value proposition:**

> Kerno does not remove management liability. For one critical service, it
> gives management a monthly exception cadence and a contemporaneous record
> so the next decision is not reconstructed after the fact.

### Slide 6: Monthly oversight, not an annual green slide

**Headline:** The meeting is exception-led. Closures need named evidence.

**Operating cycle:**

1. **Collect:** Control owners submit evidence through Kerno (upload) or
   existing inbound connectors.
2. **Link and score:** Each record is linked to named controls. Coverage is
   scored from those links.
3. **Register:** Gaps and exceptions have an owner, an age, and either
   linked evidence or an explicit gap. This register is the agenda.
4. **Review:** Monthly 90-minute session with management or the delegated
   sponsor. Only items that need a decision or a push. Recommendations on
   the table have already been human-reviewed.
5. **Preserve:** Decisions in that meeting are recorded in Kerno.
   Remediation claims are not closed on a verbal update; they need linked
   evidence.

**Evidence produced:**

- Source-linked evidence and the time it was recorded
- Coverage status and confidence per in-scope control
- Exception owner, age, and whether closure has evidence
- Monthly review note and named decisions
- Exportable evidence pack for the in-scope family

**Speaker script:**

“Traditional oversight relies on self-reporting and slides built the week
before. We change the cadence: Kerno already knows which of the in-scope
controls have evidence and which do not. The monthly review is the register.
If a closure cannot be pointed at a named record, it is not closed. That is
narrower than a monitoring platform, and it is already enough to stop the
usual drift between audits — on this service, for these controls.”

## Suggested outcome metrics

Use these to prove the cycle is working. Do not reduce compliance to a
single score. Do not report metrics the copilot cannot support.

- Percentage of in-scope controls with current linked evidence
- Count of unlinked evidence records (orphans made visible)
- Number of open exceptions with a named owner
- Median age of open high exceptions
- Percentage of remediation closures that have linked evidence
- Percentage of open recommendations that have been human-reviewed
- Days from pack to recorded management decision
- Number of in-scope controls still scored gap
- One evidence-pack export successfully produced

Do not promise: independent verification rates, automatically reopened
acceptances, training completion, or “unsupported green statuses eliminated
across the estate.” Those belong to a later product.
